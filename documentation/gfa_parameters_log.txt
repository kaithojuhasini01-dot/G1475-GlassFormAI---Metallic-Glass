## 1. What was done:
We calculated the Gamma (\(\gamma\)) parameter, which is a known indicator of Glass-Forming Ability (GFA). The formula is:
**\(\gamma = T_x / (T_g + T_l)\)**

## 2. Implementation:
Instead of using experimental temperatures, we used the **Predicted Temperatures** generated by our ETR models. This simulates how our model would screen completely new, unseen alloys. 

Because the test sets for \(T_l\), \(T_x\), and \(T_g\) had slightly different numbers of records (due to missing experimental values in the original dataset), we used a `dropna` function to remove any rows where \(T_l\) was missing. This ensured that Gamma was only calculated for alloys that had all three predicted temperatures. The final calculation was performed on 165 alloys using the `calculate_gfa.py` script.

## 3. Expected Output (Top 5 Alloys by Gamma)
The script successfully calculated Gamma for all 165 alloys. Below are the top 5 alloys predicted to have the highest Glass-Forming Ability:

## 4. Significance and Limitations
**Significance:** A higher Gamma value indicates better thermal stability of the supercooled liquid and a higher resistance to crystallization. In the literature, a Gamma value above 0.5 is considered good, and our top alloy reached an exceptional **0.7914**.

**Limitations :** A calculated GFA indicator does not by itself establish experimental glass-forming ability. The predicted Gamma values are based on our ETR model's estimates and must be verified through physical lab experiments. Furthermore, the limitation of our dataset (containing only successful glass formers) means the model cannot predict alloys that will fail to form glass.
